01 / THE CHALLENGE

Global ambition. Local complexity.

As Principal Product Manager, I owned the end-to-end expansion strategy across Africa, the Middle East, Europe and Singapore. The work required market-entry decisions that considered the opportunity in each market alongside the commercial and operational implications of entering it.

The central challenge was to choose how to expand, which partner to work with, and whether the partner’s fees were justified by the opportunity available in that market. Payment performance and compliance issues were also important outcome measures.

THE PRODUCT QUESTIONWhich markets should we enter, through which partners, and at what commercial cost—while monitoring payment outcomes and compliance exposure?

02 / PRODUCT STRATEGY

Choose where to compete, which rails to use, and what to standardise.

The expansion strategy centred on comparing market opportunity with the cost and feasibility of entry. The core decision dimensions were:

  • Market opportunity: Assess the potential opportunity within each target market as an input to expansion prioritisation.
  • Partner selection: Compare potential partners and the fees they charge against the opportunity available in the market.
  • Expansion approach: Decide how to enter each market, weighing the commercial case against the practical requirements of execution.
  • Risk and performance visibility: Include payment volume, success rate, failure rate and compliance issues among the measures used to assess outcomes.

This frames market expansion as a portfolio of commercial decisions: not only where to expand, but whether the partner economics and opportunity justify entry. Specific market rankings, partner names, fee comparisons and decision outcomes still need to be added if they can be substantiated and disclosed.
