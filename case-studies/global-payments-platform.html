01 / THE CHALLENGE

Expanding payment reach without treating every market as the same.

Cross-border payment products must reconcile a global customer proposition with local market realities. Payment schemes differ in reach, clearing and settlement behaviour, operating windows, partner coverage, compliance obligations and implementation effort.

The product challenge was to shape a coherent expansion approach across payment methods such as ACH and SWIFT, while accounting for local rails and the dependencies required to make each market operationally viable.

Validate before publishing: the exact business objective, target customer segments, starting-state constraints and markets included in this initiative.

02 / PRODUCT STRATEGY

Build a repeatable market-expansion model.

The product narrative should show how market selection and payment-rail choices were evaluated against customer demand, corridor coverage, partner readiness, compliance requirements, delivery complexity and expected commercial value.

  • Market and corridor prioritisation: establish a transparent way to compare demand, strategic value, feasibility and time to launch.
  • Rail selection: determine where ACH, SWIFT or a local payment method best fits the use case, considering reach, speed, cost, availability and operational constraints.
  • Partner dependencies: map the capabilities, responsibilities, service expectations and integration requirements of banking and payment partners.
  • Reusable platform capabilities: separate common product and integration patterns from market-specific requirements to avoid unnecessary one-off delivery.

Validate before publishing: which of these decisions you personally owned, which frameworks or artefacts were used, and which alternatives were rejected.

03 / EXECUTION & GOVERNANCE

Turn a market plan into a launch-ready service.

A payment capability is not launch-ready when an API integration merely works. Product, Engineering, Compliance, Risk, Treasury, Operations and Commercial teams need a shared view of readiness and ownership.

The case study should explain how requirements were aligned across teams; how partner and regulatory dependencies were managed; and how launch criteria covered payment initiation, status visibility, exception handling, reconciliation, settlement and operational support.

  • Define market-level requirements and explicit launch acceptance criteria.
  • Track integration, compliance, operational and partner dependencies through a common delivery plan.
  • Establish post-launch monitoring for payment outcomes, failure reasons, exceptions and operational incidents.
  • Feed launch learnings back into reusable product patterns and the next market's prioritisation.

Validate before publishing: the actual governance model, your role in decision-making, launch sequence and examples that can be disclosed publicly.

05 / LEADERSHIP REFLECTION

What this demonstrates about product leadership.

The strongest version of this story will show how you balanced market opportunity with delivery capacity, made explicit trade-offs, aligned stakeholders with different incentives, and converted a fragmented set of payment capabilities into a coherent product proposition.

Close with a specific learning: what you would repeat, what you would change, and how the experience shaped your approach to platform strategy or international expansion. Add this reflection only after grounding it in your own experience.
